# Founder's Notes

This file preserves the reasoning that produced the architecture.

It is intentionally informal.

The goal is preserving intellectual continuity.

---

# Origin Story

The architecture did not begin with governance.

It did not begin with authority.

It did not begin with legitimacy.

It began with a recurring observation:

Every time a boundary was secured,
the failure appeared one layer above it.

The ghost kept moving.

---

# The Ghost Theory

Decision systems defend decisions.

Attackers target mutations.

Mutation systems defend mutations.

Attackers target governance.

Governance systems defend governance.

Attackers target authority.

Authority systems defend authority.

Attackers target legitimacy.

Legitimacy systems defend legitimacy.

Attackers target ontology.

The ghost is the dependency one layer above the current boundary.

---

# BBIS Moment

The breakthrough occurred when the goal changed.

Old goal:

Find the irreversible mutation boundary.

New goal:

Carry the invariant until the ghost appears.

Instead of chasing the ghost,
force the ghost to interact with the invariant.

Boundary-to-boundary invariant survival became more important than discovering the final boundary.

---

# Major Realization

The architecture cannot eliminate uncertainty.

The architecture cannot eliminate unknown attacks.

The architecture cannot eliminate the ghost.

The architecture can force contradictions to leave evidence.

---

# Why Mycelium

Mycelium survives local destruction.

No single strand is the network.

Continuity emerges from relationships.

The architecture should behave similarly.

---

# Rule Change

Traditional security asks:

How do we stop attacks?

LMA asks:

How do we ensure attacks improve the system more than the attacker?

---

# Future Warning

Do not optimize for elegance.

Do not optimize for complexity.

Do not optimize for immortality.

Optimize for survivability.

---

# Final Reminder

The objective is not perfection.

The objective is preserving enough truth that future systems can learn from failure.

If execution survives, continue.

If execution dies, preserve evidence.

Both outcomes preserve continuity.
